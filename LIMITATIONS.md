# Limitations

- **Sample gates can pass falsely.** Each fact has 8 training, 20 probe and 50 test samples. Probe sets are repeatedly reused for gating and repair decisions, so they are adaptive validation sets, not untouched evidence of general retention. Test sets are separate and do not control progression; they are finite estimates too. Strict 100/100 describes probe acceptance, not universal or perfect retention.
- **Budget and method limits.** A first unresolved interference event ends strict RGLL. Stops depend on the world, initialization, optimizer, replay, thresholds and loop budget. Five seeds are descriptive evidence, not a precise estimate of stochastic risk. Changing width changes network initialization and parameter count; changing the seed changes both world and initialization. The stored results do not isolate initialization effects in a factorial experiment.
- **Synthetic scope.** These are noisy key-to-value facts, a two-hidden-layer MLP, Adam and stored replay samples. They are not language-model, real-world, lifelong, or general-memory tests. All-fact checks scale with the accepted set. Finite true-key classifier checks reported in the historical README do not prove population-level separability or intrinsic capacity.
- **Comparison scope.** Modified RGLL can defer and flag failures. About 44% of calibrated stress facts were flagged on average. Its baseline ablation must not be presented as a strict-RGLL result. Seed 0 was used for stress calibration. Baseline allocations use RGLL's measured budget and deferrals, rounding and an estimated number of visits; they are not independently optimized baselines or exact realized cost matches. The final-check revisit baseline nevertheless beat modified RGLL on every stored seed. No corresponding strict baseline ablation is supplied.
- **Compute is a proxy.** Recorded compute is `3 * training_samples + probe_samples`. It excludes independent test evaluation and other overhead, and is neither FLOPs nor wall time. It does not account for model width, so compute units across widths are not hardware-cost-equivalent. Joint training is a nonsequential reference, not a mathematical upper bound.
- **Incomplete historical reproducibility.** Configurations record hashes for four strict-related files, not the complete dependency graph. Core strict/data/learner hashes match the published source; older runner hashes do not. Their historical bytes are absent. The original requirements were unpinned; observed package versions were captured at publication, not sealed before training. Different versions or hardware may change results. No reproduction training was performed for publication.
- **Failures remain visible.** The 1,000-fact/128-unit/200-loop log ends with a Windows console encoding error after result tables were saved; historical notes say the plot was generated separately without retraining. The interrupted initial stress runs have narrative evidence but no separate raw outputs in the supplied folder. Neither missing outputs nor missing source revisions were reconstructed.
- **Priority and attribution.** Jonathan Harriss supplied the concept and strict hypothesis; Claude Opus implemented the experiments. No literature review or novelty assessment was performed. Some historical comments claim the modified procedure advances only on gate passes, or interpret loop saturation as a full network. The implementation and these limitations take precedence over those descriptions.
